# Doodle to ASCII

A simple drawing app that turns a doodle into ASCII art for a digital journal or text.

## The Unique Kernel
A low-barrier doodle editor turns a personal sketch into handmade, customizable ASCII art that can replace a premade sticker or emoji in a text.

## Who It's For
Younger people who use premade stickers when texting and want a handmade, customizable alternative for texts or digital journals.

## The Core Loop
Open the doodle editor, draw with a fixed-size black pen, erase as needed, generate one ASCII image, and download it as a `.txt` file to use as a personal sticker or emoji replacement.

## Inspiration & Identity
The app should have the feel of early web aesthetics and drawing web games such as scribble.io: simple, intuitive, and homemade. Keep the interface palette black and white. The generated art should follow the sample you shared: a dense, detailed ASCII image built from varied characters, using character density for shading and recognizable contours. This is a reference for the output art only, not a model for the app interface.

## Why This Matters to the Learner
The learner wants to better understand how to use AI effectively for coding.

## What "Working" Looks Like
In a short demo, someone opens the editor, makes a doodle with the pen and eraser, generates a single ASCII version, and downloads it as a text file.

## The POC Boundary
Build only the drawing-to-ASCII path: a doodle canvas with a fixed-size black pen and eraser, one generated ASCII result, and a `.txt` download. Keep the experience small enough to demonstrate in about a minute and target 2–4 hours of active work.

## Later
Image import and image-file export; additional drawing tools, colors, or adjustable tool sizes; multiple ASCII complexity levels; copying the result directly to the clipboard.

## Explicitly Cut
No image import, shape or fill tools, adjustable pen or eraser size, grayscale color selection, or multiple ASCII renders in this proof of concept. Keeping one simple drawing-to-download flow protects the low-barrier idea and the hackathon-sized scope.
