Proof of concept / 01

Doodle to ASCII

A simple drawing app for making handmade, customizable ASCII art to use instead of premade stickers or emoji.

The one-minute proof

From doodle to shareable text

Young creator → doodle with black pen / erase → generate one ASCII render → download `.txt`.

A small, screen-demonstrable loop. No image import or alternate render levels in this version.

The distinctive idea

A low-barrier doodle editor turns a personal sketch into handmade, customizable ASCII art that can replace a premade sticker or emoji in a text.

Who it's for

Younger people who use premade stickers when texting and want a handmade, customizable alternative for texts or digital journals.

Look and feel

The app should feel like early web aesthetics and drawing web games such as scribble.io: simple, intuitive, and homemade. Keep the interface palette black and white. The generated art should follow the sample you shared: a dense, detailed ASCII image built from varied characters, using character density for shading and recognizable contours. This is an output-art reference only, not a model for the app interface.

Why it matters

The learner wants to better understand how to use AI effectively for coding.

Keep the experiment small

Scope boundary

Now / in the proof of concept

A doodle canvas with a fixed-size black pen and eraser, one generated ASCII result, and a `.txt` download. A short demo should show someone drawing, generating, and downloading the result in about a minute; target 2–4 hours of active work.

Later / possible additions
  • Image import and image-file export.
  • Additional drawing tools, colors, or adjustable tool sizes.
  • Multiple ASCII complexity levels.
  • Copying the result directly to the clipboard.
Cut / not in this version

No image import, shape or fill tools, adjustable pen or eraser size, grayscale color selection, or multiple ASCII renders. Keeping one simple drawing-to-download flow protects the low-barrier idea and hackathon-sized scope.
